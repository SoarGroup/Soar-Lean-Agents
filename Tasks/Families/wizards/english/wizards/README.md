- **A-WZ1** (statements 1, 2): The eight possible patterns of spots are world objects (w-bww: A black, B white, C white); spot(c, w, colour) gives each wizard's colour in each world; view-mate(c, w, w2) holds when w2 differs from w only in c's own spot (what c cannot see). The actual world is w-bww.
- **A-WZ2** (statements 6): "B and C start marked, A unmarked" in the description is read as B and C white and A black (marked = the colour the announcement is about).
- **A-WZ3** (statements 1, 4, 5): Knowledge is explicit: knows-at(c, w, r) holds when, in world w at turn r, no world c cannot tell from w is still possible. Derived relations use negation only in their definitions; operators test them positively.
- **A-WZ4** (statements 3, 4, 5): The announcement and each turn are operators that rule out worlds (out-at(w, turn)). A world is live at turn r if it was not ruled out at an earlier turn. A stage number keeps the order; turn objects carry their number and asks(turn, wizard) says who is asked (A, B, C in that order).
- **A-WZ5** (statements 4, 5, 7): A turn records the asked wizard's answer in the actual world: if he knows, he states his colour (says(c, colour), the colour of his spot in the actual world, so it is correct). Every world where he would have answered differently is ruled out, since all hear the answer.
- **A-WZ6** (statements 7): The goal is that some wizard has stated his colour. With A black, B and C white, A and B do not know and C states white at the third turn.
